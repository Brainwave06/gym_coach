Title: Wrist Pain

Overview:
Wrist pain during gym training commonly arises from exercises that place the wrist in an extended or heavily loaded position, such as push-ups, planks, and pressing movements. It is usually related to how load is distributed through the wrist joint rather than a serious structural injury, and can often be managed effectively by adjusting hand position, load, and exercise selection.

Common Causes:
- Extended wrist position during push-ups and front-support planks under bodyweight load
- Direct pressure through the palm during heavy pressing exercises
- Insufficient warm-up before wrist-loading exercises
- Sudden increases in pressing volume or load
- Existing conditions such as tendinitis or carpal tunnel syndrome that make the wrist more sensitive to load

Exercises to Avoid:
- Standard push-ups performed directly on flat, extended wrists
- Front planks resting on the hands rather than the forearms
- Heavy barbell bench press or overhead press with poor wrist alignment
- Barbell curls that force significant wrist flexion under load
- Any wrist-loaded exercise that reproduces sharp pain

Safer Alternatives:
- Push-ups performed on push-up handles, dumbbells, or in a fist position to keep the wrist neutral
- Forearm plank instead of a front (hand-supported) plank
- Dumbbell press variations with a neutral or angled grip instead of barbell pressing
- Hammer curls or neutral-grip curls instead of standard barbell curls
- Machine-based pressing exercises, which offer more wrist support and stability

General Guidance:
Keeping the wrist in a neutral position, aligned with the forearm, is one of the most effective ways to reduce strain during pressing and weight-bearing exercises. Switching from barbell to dumbbell or machine variations often reduces wrist stress while still training the same muscle groups. A proper warm-up that includes wrist mobility work can help prepare the joint for load-bearing exercises. Training through sharp pain should be avoided, as this generally worsens irritation rather than building tolerance.

When to See a Doctor:
Consult a doctor if wrist pain is persistent, worsens over time, is accompanied by swelling or numbness, or does not improve despite modifying grip and exercise selection. This is especially important for individuals with a known condition such as arthritis, carpal tunnel syndrome, or a previous wrist injury.
